A standard vise holds a part through two flat jaws, so irregular or delicate workpieces end up gripped at a few points, where they slip or get damaged. In 2.009 Product Engineering Processes, MIT's capstone product design class, our 20-person team set out to build a vise that could hold any shape firmly without marring it. Our answer was granular jamming: a pouch filled with loose grains conforms to whatever it is pressed against, and when the pneumatic system jams the grains together, the pouch locks into that shape. Clamping force is then spread over the whole contact area rather than concentrated at a few points.

We worked through the course's cycle of "Ideate. Model. Test. Repeat." over several prototypes, moving from pouches added to an off-the-shelf vise to a self-contained product. I led the task force responsible for the vise body, which we designed around waterjet-cut steel plates welded together to house the pneumatic system and carry clamping loads. The final design pairs two removable, conformable pouches with a spring-loaded jaw that keeps them in contact, a self-regulated pneumatic system inside the body, and a control for fast actuation.

{% include figure.html src="/assets/images/revise/figures/exploded-view.webp" alt="Exploded render of the ReVise vise with labels: rapid onset actuation, spring-loaded jaw to ensure contact, conformable pouches, and self-regulated pneumatic system." caption="Exploded view of the final design, from the product sheet I designed." %}

I also built and maintained the team's master CAD assembly and produced its renders, animations, brochure, posters, and presentation slides. We presented ReVise to an audience in Kresge Auditorium in December 2021 and demonstrated it by clamping a raw egg without cracking its shell. The business case priced the vise at $450 with replacement pouches at $20 for a two-pack, aimed at roughly 100,000 workshops in the US.

The pouches are a wear part, which is why the business case includes replacement packs. The presentation recording is linked above, and ReVise begins at 2:18:38.
